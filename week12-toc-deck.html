What Is an Activity?

InterventionLiteracy classes
  • Identifying space and teachers
  • Choosing a curriculum
  • Screening students

An intervention is made of activities: the things that would actually happen.

1Week 11 said what the club should create. Today says how it would run, week to week.
2An activity is something you could see someone doing, on a day of the week.

“We use activity to describe all the specific actions that make up an intervention.”

Taplin & Clark · [TC] p. 7

The three activities are [TC] p. 7’s own: “identifying space and teachers, choosing a curriculum, screening students” ([TC] p. 7).

Four Targets for Every Sign

“Each indicator needs to have four targets: How many of who or what will reach what level by when?”

Taplin & Clark · [TC] p. 8

[TC]’s example: “the indicators for that might be to have two new computers and a new set of class books for 8 of 12 classrooms within a year” ([TC] p. 8). The workbench calls an indicator a sign that it is working.

Screen 11 · Signs

One Sign, One Sentence

The workbench reads your four targets back as one sentence:

1Choose the box on your map it shows: the long-term outcome, or a box below it.
2Say how the club would count it: a door count, a short survey, a booking list.
3Do you know the number today? If not, it goes on Find out first.

“Your indicator should make it extremely clear to everyone what you will consider success for a given outcome.” ([TC] p. 8)
